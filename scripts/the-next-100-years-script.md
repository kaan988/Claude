# THE NEXT 100 YEARS — How AI Could Change Everything (2026–2126)

**Runtime:** about 20 minutes (~3,000 words of narration at ~150 words per minute)
**Format:** Decade-by-decade journey, framed as a message from the year 2126
**Tone:** Curious and cinematic, hopeful but honest

**How to read this script**
- **[VISUAL]**: what's on screen
- **[SFX / MUSIC]**: sound cues
- **NARRATOR**: voiceover
- **[LABEL]**: on-screen tag showing how likely a prediction is: 🟢 LIKELY · 🟡 POSSIBLE · 🔴 SPECULATIVE
- A **year counter** in the top corner runs the whole video, from 2026 to 2126.

---

## COLD OPEN (0:00 – 1:30)

**[VISUAL]** Black screen. A faint heartbeat. Slowly fade into grainy black-and-white footage from the 1920s: horse carts beside early cars, gas lamps, a crowded hospital ward.

**[SFX]** Old film projector clicking.

**NARRATOR:**
In 1926, there was no television in anyone's home. There were no antibiotics. Penicillin wouldn't be discovered for another two years. No computers. No internet. No jet planes. Around the world, the average person didn't live much past their mid-thirties.

**[VISUAL]** Fast montage, accelerating: a radio, a TV, the Moon landing, a giant computer, a flip phone, a smartphone, a chatbot typing on a screen.

**NARRATOR:**
One hundred years later, we carry the sum of human knowledge in our pockets. We've walked on the Moon, mapped the human genome, and built machines that can write, speak, see, and reason.

**[VISUAL]** The montage freezes on a modern city at night. The year counter appears: **2026**.

**NARRATOR:**
That was one century. Now we're at the start of another one. And this time, we're not just building new tools.

*(beat)*

We're building something that can build tools by itself.

**[MUSIC]** Low synth swell.

**NARRATOR:**
So where does it lead? What does the world look like in 2126, after a hundred years of artificial intelligence?

**[VISUAL]** Title card: **THE NEXT 100 YEARS**

Hello. If you're watching this, you're living in the 2020s, the decade when it all started. ## CHAPTER 1 — THE 2030s: THE ASSISTANT ERA (2:30 – 5:00)

**[VISUAL]** Year counter ticks to **2030**. A bright morning in a normal apartment. A teenager talks to a small device while eating breakfast.

**NARRATOR:**
The first big change won't look like science fiction. It will look like ordinary life, just with a lot more help.

**[LABEL]** 🟢 LIKELY: AI tutors for every child

**NARRATOR:**
Picture a student in a village with no school nearby. In the 2030s, she has a personal tutor that knows exactly where she's struggling. It explains fractions ten different ways until one of them clicks. It never gets tired and never gets impatient. And it speaks her language.

For most of history, a private tutor was a luxury for the rich. Within a decade, it could be something every child on Earth has.

**[VISUAL]** Split screen: a classroom with 40 students and one teacher, next to a single student with a glowing AI companion.

**[LABEL]** 🟢 LIKELY: AI doctors in your pocket

**NARRATOR:**
The same goes for medicine. Your phone looks at a rash, listens to your cough, reads your blood test, and tells you whether you need a doctor today or can rest at home. It won't replace doctors. It will catch problems earlier, for billions of people who have never had a doctor at all.

**[VISUAL]** An office building where the desks slowly empty and AI interfaces take over the screens.

**[LABEL]** 🟢 LIKELY: AI agents doing real work

**NARRATOR:**
At work, AI stops being something you chat with and becomes something that acts for you. It books the flights, writes the code, files the taxes, answers the customers. One person with a team of AI agents can run a company that used to need fifty people.

That's exciting. It's also frightening.

**[VISUAL]** A worker staring at a laptop showing a "Position eliminated" email.

**NARRATOR:**
Because the 2030s are also when the big question hits for real: *What happens to people whose jobs a machine can do?* How we answer that question will shape the rest of the century.

**[VISUAL]** Driverless cars moving smoothly through a city intersection.

**NARRATOR:**
Meanwhile, the streets get quieter. Self-driving cars spread from city to city. Traffic deaths, which kill over a million people a year today, start to fall.

## CHAPTER 2 — THE 2040s: THE MEDICINE REVOLUTION (5:00 – 7:30)

**[VISUAL]** Year counter: **2040**. Microscopic view: colorful proteins folding and spinning in 3D.

**NARRATOR:**
Inside every cell of your body, millions of tiny machines called proteins do almost all the work of keeping you alive. For fifty years, figuring out the shape of a single protein could take a scientist years.

Then, in the early 2020s, an AI predicted the shapes of almost every protein known to science. Its creators won a Nobel Prize for it.

**[VISUAL]** Animated "database" of hundreds of millions of proteins filling the screen.

**NARRATOR:**
That was just the beginning. By the 2040s, AI isn't only reading biology. It's writing it.

**[LABEL]** 🟢 LIKELY: AI-designed medicines

**NARRATOR:**
Developing a new drug used to take more than ten years and billions of dollars, and most attempts failed. AI can now test millions of molecules in a simulation before anyone touches a test tube. Drugs for rare diseases that no company could afford to research suddenly become possible.

**[LABEL]** 🟡 POSSIBLE: Most cancers become manageable

**NARRATOR:**
Cancer starts to look different too. Your tumor's DNA is read, an AI designs a treatment just for you, and a personalized vaccine trains your immune system to hunt it down. Not every cancer is cured, but many become something you live with, like diabetes, instead of something you die from.

**[VISUAL]** Hospital scene: a doctor and patient looking at a holographic body scan, with tiny areas highlighted long before symptoms appear.

**NARRATOR:**
And the biggest change is timing. Medicine shifts from fixing you when you're sick to stopping you from getting sick at all. Continuous monitoring, early warnings, and treatment starting years before a disease would have shown up.

**[VISUAL]** Robots in a home: one folding laundry, one helping an elderly man stand up.

**[LABEL]** 🟡 POSSIBLE: Humanoid robots in homes

**NARRATOR:**
At home, the AI gets a body. Humanoid robots cook, clean, and care for the elderly. In aging countries like Japan, Germany, and South Korea, where there aren't enough young people to look after the old, this may not be a luxury. It may be a necessity.

**[VISUAL]** A glowing ring of plasma inside a fusion reactor.

**[LABEL]** 🟡 POSSIBLE: Fusion power comes online

**NARRATOR:**
And then there's energy. Fusion, the power source of the Sun, has always been "thirty years away." But AI is already learning to control the superheated plasma inside fusion reactors. If fusion works, the world gets almost limitless clean energy. And cheap energy changes everything else.

## CHAPTER 3 — THE 2050s: UPGRADING THE HUMAN (7:30 – 9:30)

**[VISUAL]** Year counter: **2050**. A 70-year-old woman running a marathon, smiling.

**NARRATOR:**
By the middle of the century, the most important patient isn't a disease. It's aging itself.

**[LABEL]** 🟡 POSSIBLE: Aging slowed down

**NARRATOR:**
Scientists have long suspected that aging isn't one thing but many kinds of damage piling up in our cells over time. With AI analyzing biology at a scale no human team could, some of that damage might become repairable.

Picture being 60 with the body of a 40-year-old. Not living forever, just staying healthy much, much longer.

**[VISUAL]** A person with a thin, almost invisible device behind the ear, typing on a virtual screen with their thoughts.

**[LABEL]** 🟡 POSSIBLE: Brain-computer interfaces

**NARRATOR:**
Brain-computer interfaces are already helping paralyzed people move cursors and write sentences using only their thoughts. By the 2050s, they could let people who are blind see, let people who are paralyzed walk, and maybe, for those who choose it, let anyone talk to an AI as fast as they can think.

**[VISUAL]** Two people from different countries talking. Their words appear in each other's language in real time.

**[LABEL]** 🟢 LIKELY: The end of language barriers

**NARRATOR:**
And for the first time in history, language stops dividing us. A farmer in Brazil and an engineer in Korea have a conversation, each hearing the other in their own language, in their own voice, instantly. The Tower of Babel, rebuilt.

**[VISUAL]** Slow push-in on a person's face, half in shadow.

**NARRATOR:**
But here the questions get personal. If you can upgrade your memory, should you? If the rich can afford a longer, sharper life, do we end up with two kinds of humans? Technology doesn't answer these questions. We have to.

## CHAPTER 4 — THE 2060s: REPAIRING THE PLANET (9:30 – 11:30)

**[VISUAL]** Year counter: **2060**. Drone shot over a dry, cracked landscape that turns green as the camera moves forward.

**NARRATOR:**
The 21st century has a deadline: climate change. And by the 2060s, AI may be one of our strongest tools for fighting it.

**[LABEL]** 🟡 POSSIBLE: AI-run power grids and climate repair

**NARRATOR:**
AI runs the power grid, balancing solar, wind, and fusion second by second, so nothing goes to waste. AI-designed materials pull carbon out of the air. Battery chemistries invented by machines store power for weeks.

**[VISUAL]** Ocean scene: underwater drones planting coral, schools of fish returning.

**NARRATOR:**
In the oceans, fleets of autonomous robots clean up plastic and restore coral reefs. In the forests, AI satellites spot illegal logging and wildfires the moment they start.

**[VISUAL]** Inside a towering vertical farm with endless rows of green lit by purple LEDs.

**[LABEL]** 🟢 LIKELY: Food grown in new ways

**NARRATOR:**
Food changes too. Vertical farms in the middle of cities grow crops with a fraction of the water. Meat grown from cells, not animals. Crops designed to survive drought and heat. The land we once needed for farming can slowly go back to the wild.

**[VISUAL]** A city street with no cars parked, wide sidewalks, and trees everywhere.

**NARRATOR:**
And the cities themselves start to breathe. Fewer cars, because shared robotaxis are always nearby. Parking lots turned into parks. Buildings that heat and cool themselves.

**[VISUAL]** Pull back to reveal Earth from orbit, the clouds swirling.

**NARRATOR:**
None of this is guaranteed. AI itself needs enormous amounts of energy, and technology can't replace political will. But for the first time, we may have the tools to not just slow the damage, but undo it.

## CHAPTER 5 — THE 2070s: THE SCIENCE EXPLOSION (11:30 – 13:30)

**[VISUAL]** Year counter: **2070**. A vast, quiet laboratory with no humans, only robotic arms running thousands of experiments at once.

**NARRATOR:**
For all of history, scientific progress has moved at the speed of human thought. A scientist has an idea, runs an experiment, waits, and tries again. A lifetime might produce a handful of real discoveries.

**[LABEL]** 🟡 POSSIBLE: AI scientists

**NARRATOR:**
Now imagine millions of AI scientists working day and night, each reading every paper ever written, forming hypotheses, and running experiments in automated labs. Discoveries that would have taken a century might take a year.

**[VISUAL]** Text on screen scrolling faster and faster: "New superconductor discovered… New antibiotic class… Dark matter candidate… New mathematical proof…"

**NARRATOR:**
By the 2070s, the problem isn't making discoveries. It's keeping up with them. Humans might start to feel like tourists in their own science, needing AI just to explain what AI has found.

**[VISUAL]** The Moon's surface. Robots building a dome-shaped base using lunar soil.

**[LABEL]** 🟡 POSSIBLE: Robot-built bases on the Moon and Mars

**NARRATOR:**
And the frontier moves off-planet. Space is deadly for humans, but robots don't need air, food, or sleep. AI-controlled machines go first, mining ice, printing buildings from moon dust, and preparing shelters. When the first humans arrive to stay, the lights are already on.

**[VISUAL]** Mars at sunset. A small human figure stands at a window looking out at the red landscape.

**NARRATOR:**
A child born on Mars in the 2070s might never have seen a blue sky with their own eyes. Their whole world would have been built for them by machines.

## CHAPTER 6 — 2080–2110: WHAT DOES IT MEAN TO BE HUMAN? (13:30 – 15:30)

**[VISUAL]** Year counter accelerates: **2080… 2090… 2100**. Visuals become more abstract and dreamlike.

**NARRATOR:**
The further we go, the blurrier the future gets. So now we move into the boldest ideas, the ones that might sound impossible today.

**[VISUAL]** A 115-year-old man playing football with his great-great-grandchildren.

**[LABEL]** 🔴 SPECULATIVE: Lifespans beyond 120

**NARRATOR:**
If the medical revolutions of the 2040s and 2050s keep compounding, living past 120 could become normal. Imagine five generations of a family at the same dinner table. Imagine starting a new career at 90.

**[VISUAL]** A world with no offices: people painting, building, teaching, exploring, playing music.

**[LABEL]** 🟡 POSSIBLE: The end of work as we know it

**NARRATOR:**
If AI and robots can do almost any job better and cheaper, then work as we know it fades away. Some people imagine a world of abundance, where everyone gets a share of what the machines produce, and people work because they want to, not because they have to.

But that raises a question humanity has never faced before: *If you don't need to work to survive, what gives your life meaning?*

**[VISUAL]** An elderly woman talking to a glowing hologram of her late husband.

**[LABEL]** 🔴 SPECULATIVE: Digital memories of loved ones

**NARRATOR:**
Some might choose to leave behind AI versions of themselves, trained on their words, their voice, their memories, so their grandchildren can still talk to them. Is that comfort? Or is it a way of never letting go?

**[VISUAL]** A slow zoom into a human brain that turns into a galaxy of glowing connections.

**[LABEL]** 🔴 SPECULATIVE: Understanding consciousness

**NARRATOR:**
And then there's the deepest mystery of all. What is consciousness? Could a machine ever truly feel something? By the 2100s, AI might help us map every connection in the human brain. And we might have to decide whether some AIs deserve rights of their own.

**[VISUAL]** A tiny probe with a solar sail, catching starlight as it leaves the solar system.

**[LABEL]** 🔴 SPECULATIVE: Probes to other stars

**NARRATOR:**
At the edge of the century, the first AI probes might be on their way to another star. Too far for humans, too slow for a lifetime. But an AI doesn't mind waiting.

## CHAPTER 7 — THE OTHER FUTURE (15:30 – 18:00)

**[MUSIC]** The music cuts out abruptly.

**[VISUAL]** Static. The year counter glitches and rewinds to **2026**.

**NARRATOR:**
Everything you've seen so far is the good version. But there is another version of this story.

**[VISUAL]** The same city from the opening, but darker. Surveillance cameras on every corner. Screens everywhere.

**NARRATOR:**
In this version, the power of AI ends up in the hands of a few companies or a few governments. AI watches everything you do, decides what you see, and knows what you'll think before you think it.

**[VISUAL]** Crowds of people standing still, faces lit by phones.

**NARRATOR:**
In this version, jobs disappear faster than society can adapt. The wealth created by machines goes to the people who own the machines, and everyone else is left behind.

**[VISUAL]** Distorted, flickering news clips. The same politician's face saying two opposite things.

**NARRATOR:**
In this version, deepfakes and AI-generated lies flood the world until no one can tell what's real anymore. Trust breaks down. Democracy struggles.

**[VISUAL]** A server room, cold and blue, the hum growing louder.

**NARRATOR:**
And in the most serious version, we build systems smarter than ourselves before we know how to make sure they want what we want. Many leading AI researchers take this risk seriously, and they're working on it right now. It's called AI safety, or alignment.

**[VISUAL]** Back to black. Silence for two seconds.

**NARRATOR:**
None of this is meant to scare you. It's meant to remind you that the future isn't something that just *happens*. It's something we *choose*. The same technology that could cure disease could be used to control people. The same AI that could end poverty could make inequality worse than ever.

**[VISUAL]** A fork in a road, seen from above. One path green and bright, the other grey.

**NARRATOR:**
The difference between these two futures won't come down to the technology. It will come down to the decisions people make in the next few decades: the laws we write, the companies we support, the questions we ask, and whether we insist that AI serves everyone and not just a few.

**[MUSIC]** A single piano note returns.

Here in 2126, people still wake up in the morning. They still fall in love, argue with their families, and laugh at bad jokes. Children still ask "why?" a thousand times a day.

A hundred years ago, people in 1926 couldn't imagine your world. And you probably can't fully imagine ours.

That's okay. You don't need to see the whole future.

You just need to remember that it starts with you. With the choices your generation makes, right now, in these first few years.

The next hundred years haven't been written yet.

So, what do you want them to look like?

## Production notes

- **Pacing:** Each chapter runs 2–2.5 minutes. If the voiceover runs long, the easiest cuts are the space section in Chapter 5 and the consciousness section in Chapter 6.
- **Recurring visual:** Return to the same street corner in every chapter (2026 → 2030 → 2040 … → 2126) so viewers see the world change in one place.
- **Music arc:** Hopeful and building (Chapters 1–6), silence and tension (Chapter 7), then quiet and emotional (Chapter 8).
- **Fact-check before publishing:** The historical claims (first TV demo in 1926, penicillin in 1928, the AlphaFold Nobel Prize in 2024, about 1.2 million road deaths a year, AI controlling fusion plasma in experiments) are accurate as of 2026, but double-check any numbers you show on screen.
- **Labels:** Keep the 🟢🟡🔴 labels consistent. Viewers trust a video more when it admits what's uncertain, and the labels give people something to argue about in the comments.
